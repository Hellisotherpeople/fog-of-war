# Longer lives without safer bullets

These are design proposals, not changes to health, enemy accuracy, wound severity or death rules.
The aim is to give a soldier more informed decisions before exposure, and a credible route back to safety.

| Proposal | Existing systems it can use | Why it could help | Constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choose an approach or muster start | Scenario briefings, rear sectors, deployment and travel | Start near the platoon's rally point, check kit, locate aid, then choose a covered approach. Some operations can still start under fire. | The front keeps moving. A longer approach costs time and can miss an opportunity; no protected spawn bubble. |
| Make the next dangerous commitment legible | Visible cover, sound marks, suppression, stance and timed inventory actions | Before a long exposed crossing or six-second search, show what the soldier already knows: open ground, nearby incoming fire, occupied hands. | No hidden enemy positions, predictions of incoming rounds or automatic dodges. Player-controlled warnings, not constant modal interruptions. |
| Make buddy drills easier to issue | Existing follow, cover, suppression, smoke and bound orders | A clear “cover while I cross / help me withdraw” command makes squad support practical before a player is isolated. | Friends need line of sight, ammunition, morale and a reachable position. Orders can fail under fire. |
| Show an aid and withdrawal plan in the briefing | Aid posts, medics, finite medical stocks, stretcher teams and permitted withdrawal | Players know which way to crawl and whom to call before bleeding becomes a crisis. | Only known aid locations; stocks can run out and routes can be cut. Carrying or treating casualties still takes time. |
| Explain a death with the preceding decisions | Existing hit causes, messages, visibility, load, wounds and orders | A short after-action timeline can connect death to an exposed reload, an untreated wound, a silhouette or a compromised identity. | Separate witnessed facts from inference. Do not reveal an unseen shooter's exact position. |
| Teach through a short rear-area drill | Movement, stance, inventory, aiming, smoke and first aid | A skippable drill makes the key survival actions familiar before a first frontline deployment. | Ordinary rules throughout. Training targets replace a live enemy only within the explicitly selected drill. |

I would start with the approach choice and the aid/withdrawal briefing, then improve buddy commands.
These create more opportunities to survive through preparation and teamwork. They preserve the possibility
that a single bad exposure kills an experienced, well-equipped soldier.

Current mechanics already support prone movement, terrain cover, suppression, smoke, medics, aid posts,
stretcher evacuation, and continuing the campaign through succession. The new search-area orders,
visible corpse equipment, readable inventory controls and explainable identity checks reduce avoidable
confusion, but none establishes a minimum lifespan or guarantees an escape.
