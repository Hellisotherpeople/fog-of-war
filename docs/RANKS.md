# Careers and national ranks

`fow/data/rank_profiles.py` separates a rank's identity, branch, seniority and command authority
from the older 0–18 tactical scale. Two ranks can share a numeric grade without becoming the
same rank. Saves retain the identity, and repeated appointments to an equal grade do not provide
an unlimited source of promotion rewards.

The national army, naval and air ladders remain available through their highest modeled ranks.
A soldier can stay in the senior enlisted career, accept a warrant or specialist appointment
where that service has one, or explicitly accept a commission. A commission is a choice, not
an automatic consequence of reaching the top enlisted grade. Duties and support allocations
are separate choices. Specialist rank does not automatically grant line command.

The additional branches include:

- US Army technician grades, Master Sergeant and First Sergeant appointments, the pre-1941
  warrant, WOJG and CWO, and Army Air Forces Flight Officer. These are wartime titles rather
  than the modern W-1 through W-5 system.
- British/Commonwealth Army WOIII, WOII and WOI, with company/regimental appointments;
  RAF warrant officer and the RCAF's two warrant classes.
- The Indian Army's Jemadar, Subedar and Subedar Major as Viceroy's commissioned officers,
  with authority limited to Indian units. These are distinct from British warrants and King's
  commissioned officers.
- US Navy specialty warrants and chief warrants, including the specialties added in 1942;
  Royal Navy specialty warrants and commissioned warrants.
- Japanese Army warrant officer, French aspirant, Polish chorąży, Chinese warrant and
  Hungarian officer-candidate distinctions. German junior enlisted ranks and Soviet senior
  lieutenant no longer disappear into duplicate numeric grades.

Availability dates apply to the explicitly dated branches. The campaign still compresses
training, personnel administration and appointments so a playable career can reach the top.
High command is an alternate-history possibility, not a claim that battlefield merit alone
historically appointed a five-star general. The common command scale remains an abstraction;
this is not an exhaustive catalog of every regimental title, rating, temporary rank or change
in military law across all countries and dates. National rank data can be extended without
rewriting the command AI.

- [US Army Center of Military History, *The Story of the Noncommissioned Officer Corps*](https://history.army.mil/portals/143/Images/Publications/catalog/70-38.pdf): wartime enlisted grades and technician insignia.
- [US Army JAG School, *Legal Administrator Deskbook* (2021)](https://www.jagcnet.army.mil/tjaglcs/Portals/1004/Periodicals/Deskbooks%20and%20Handbooks/Law%20Office%20Management%20Deskbooks/2021%20Legal%20Administrator%20Deskbook.pdf): the August 1941 WOJG/CWO legislation and technical appointments.
- [Naval History and Heritage Command, historical warrant classifications](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/h/historical-approach-to-warrant-officer-classifications.html): specialty titles and the 1942 additions.

- [Royal Canadian Air Force, historical rank examination](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/maple-leaf/rcaf/migration/2014/a-return-to-the-royal-canadian-air-force-ranks-a-historical-examination.html): wartime air-force rank structure.
- [New Zealand Ministry for Culture and Heritage, British India](https://nzhistory.govt.nz/war/british-india-facts): Indian commissioned-officer structure.
- [Polish legislation, 29 March 1963](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/1963/78/text.html): the later warrant corps and starszy chorąży; the latter is excluded from wartime progression.
