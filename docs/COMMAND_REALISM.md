# Command, engineering and information

The ground campaign separates what happened from what headquarters knows. These systems apply to both armies; the player also has personnel records, service destinations and a carried map.

## Using the systems

- **New game → Vehicle → Position:** select an exact ground vehicle or emplaced gun and a modeled crew station, or a passenger seat where available. Lists use the battle date. Normal issue automatically chooses a listed operating army and its side; a compatible chosen army is preserved. A Tiger II therefore selects Germany / Axis, including when the battle is random. To create an American crew in a German tank, explicitly select **Vehicle use → Captured equipment**, then your nation and model. Foreign captured starts use the existing unfamiliar-controls and recognition rules. Changing the model clears an incompatible seat. The assignment takes precedence over random solo, evasion and prisoner openings. Tank-crew roles also receive a tank when the random battlefield contains none. Aircraft, ships and water-only landing craft still use their existing service/scenario setup.
- **C → unit or formation:** appoint an acting commander, transfer subordinate units between commands, or give a standing mission. Appointments change command responsibility without giving out ranks. Rank, national command authority and communications are checked again when the instruction arrives.
- **Standing mission:** seize and consolidate, defend, screen/report, or reserve/support. Choose a maneuver radius and a loss threshold. Subordinates can reorganize, fetch ammunition locally, choose an approach, support a neighboring unit and dig in on arrival. Explicit orders remain in force until replaced or released. Men can still break under fire.
- **C → engineers → Engineer works:** select a project and its site. Eligible workers need tools, a clear site, finite materials and time. Basic cover can be built by equipped infantry; skilled works need engineers, Seabees or fitters. The work party carries materials from a nearby depot, workshop or stocked truck. Fighting, suppression, casualties, exhaustion and blocked sites interrupt work. Delivered material stays at the site; dead carriers do not deliver their loads.
- **C → Staff, supply and engineer works:** inspect local work parties, request a truck, and, at HQ, inspect stores and civilian ration provision.
- **T / Enter:** inspect and follow workshop instructions or a higher-headquarters conference order. Hull damage needs a workshop. Fitters can service components in a quiet location with physical spares, including damaged radios. Nearby friendlies can report a disabled tank even when its own set is out. Personal damaged kit can be serviced near a quartermaster, armourer or mechanic while parts last.
- **T / Enter also lists reporting errands automatically:** an after-action debrief after substantial reportable service, a presentation when a decoration is approved, and an intelligence hand-in when carrying captured papers. These have no artificial deadline or failure penalty. Operational orders can defer them. Destinations use the nearest known suitable surviving staff member or a marked post; the next sector exit is marked for distant posts. Handing over papers requires the actual friendly intelligence officer or quartermaster within speaking distance. Your own army's papers do not earn captured-intelligence rewards.
- **X:** select intelligence, a quartermaster, medical staff, fitters or an HQ desk to add a saved visit to **T**. The HUD arrow and **Enter** follow that same destination. You can keep several visits in the orders book; they complete when you reach and speak to the contact, or you can clear your selected service visits from X. Higher operational orders can defer a visit. This uses visible people and staff at known posts; it does not expose hidden enemy units. The same menu contains the previous terrain passability overlay.
- **Q / talk to the adjutant:** at HQ, submit an after-action report, check career eligibility, train leadership, request support or wait. Elsewhere, this adds the reporting errand to T. Walk or drive within speaking distance of a surviving HQ adjutant, clerk, operations or intelligence officer. A destroyed or abandoned command post cannot conduct the interview.
- **m / G:** use a dated map sheet and situation reports. Visit a friendly HQ to copy the latest received reports. A working HQ refreshes the sheet once a minute; that does not make the underlying reports instantaneous. Captured maps contain information from when their bearer received them.

An assigned workshop or service post stays selected through detours and sector crossings. New closer locations do not redirect an existing assignment; a destroyed post or a received report that it is no longer friendly can require a new destination. Exit waypoints stay fixed while you approach, including when your own vehicle occupies the marked spot. Service driving uses the same arrival radius as the order, rather than stopping short at a nearby pathfinding goal. Selecting a service again in X explicitly requests the nearest currently known contact.

## What changes the simulation

### Orders and national doctrine

The standing mission sits above a unit's immediate movement/fire order. Local sightings inform the leader only within voice distance or over a working net. A reserve needs nearby contact or connected radios to respond to another unit. National profiles supply terminology, preferred maneuver and the default latitude: US fire and maneuver; German Schwerpunkt/mission direction; Commonwealth fire and movement; Japanese envelopment; Soviet successive objectives; and generic fire/movement or local-initiative profiles for other armies. Heavy weapons supply fire; flanking profiles select the existing flank behavior.

These are deliberately small profiles, not a claim that every army used a single doctrine for the whole war. The Soviet, French, Italian and Finnish labels are descriptive translations, not quotations from authenticated national manuals. Profiles share much of the underlying squad AI. They are centralized in `fow/intent.py` for further battle/year-specific research.

Available HQ parties keep their officers and radios within reach of a senior commander when they can hear or contact him. Explicit player orders take precedence. Senior commanders who stop in a quiet area can cause nearby uncommitted engineers to establish a field command post. They need the same tools, supplies and working time as a player-directed work party. Rank does not conjure a building or personnel.

### Evidence and personnel

Practical skill improves from doing the job even without a witness. Official credit needs a surviving witness who saw the soldier and the action, or surviving fellow vehicle crew, and a communication route. A report is frozen when sent: a witness killed before transmission cannot send it; killing the witness afterward does not erase a report already sent.

Reports take time to process. Promotion needs reviewed evidence, time since the last promotion, a suitable vacancy and delivered personnel orders. Acting command and substantive rank are separate. Automatic kill-count rank jumps are removed. Decoration recommendations also wait for delivery. The character sheet explains the outstanding administrative requirement.

An HQ interview takes five minutes of quiet time next to personnel staff. Leaving or fighting pauses it. Surviving tank crew can give evidence in person even with a destroyed radio. Approved ground-campaign decorations are presented when you reach HQ staff; a radio message alone does not place the medal in your record. Captured documents physically handed in provide their own corroboration and add reviewed service as well as requisition credit. Repeating an interview or revisiting a citation never grants the same credit twice.

Exceptional service is banked. Promotion spends only the next appointment's requirement, preserving the surplus. There is an explicit HQ career route through NCO appointments, a possible staff-sergeant-to-second-lieutenant commission, and successive officer and general appointments through five-star rank. Leadership competence is required for commissioned appointments; an interruptible one-hour HQ course can improve it. Advancement still needs service time and a personnel decision. The campaign staff positions are an **alternate-history progression mechanism**, not a reconstruction of real vacancies, political appointment procedures or a historical private-to-five-star precedent. The accelerated timings make a long playable career possible. You can instead retain your specialist role and spend your support allocations.

Support allocations are awarded after debrief: one for each twelve points of reviewed service, additional allocations for decorations according to their level, and one, two or three for enlisted, officer or colonel-and-above promotions. These are game accounting values representing approved requests for the soldier's assigned duty, not historical rewards owed for a kill or medal. One allocation can authorize 120 requisition credit and signals/observation kit access, reserve available ammunition/fuel/spares for the current vehicle, or attach an uncommitted local friendly support unit. The quartermaster still needs stock. Vehicle supplies are reserved from finite sector stores and loaded over time at HQ; repairs still require labor and hull work still requires a workshop. Attachments move an existing unit under your mission authority, retaining its leader; they do not spawn soldiers. Training and supplies remain available without accepting a new rank.

The one-day enlisted and seven-day officer minimums, five/thirty-minute report processing, and later review/delivery delays are **game parameters**, not historical promotion regulations. Witnesses and reporting parties outside the loaded battlefield, commissioning boards, documentary medical evidence and each country's entire personnel bureaucracy are not individually simulated. Existing quest/reputation systems remain abstractions.

### Friendly fire and rehabilitation

Accidental blast, stray rounds, tracks and autonomous crew fire do not reduce standing, cause disciplinary strikes, or mark the player as a murderer. The casualties still happen. A short warning is limited to once per thirty seconds. An empty battlefield is not evidence of intent: an enemy dying before nearby friendlies does not turn the shell into an intentional attack.

Civilian losses still reduce local goodwill and are recorded as casualties, but no longer automatically deduct military merit. A witnessed deliberate attack on civilians can produce a disciplinary report under the same evidence rules.

A disciplinary report requires a player-controlled attack aimed at a visible friendly, away from close enemy fighting, and a surviving comrade who could see both the attack and its outcome. A burst or explosion is one incident regardless of fragments or casualties. Grenades and mortar shells carry the original aim and witness evidence through flight and save/load. Another harmful, witnessed, deliberate attack issued after the warning can trigger hostility. Simply remaining armed or sitting in a tank after an accusation does not trigger execution. Surrender suspends command duties pending an inquiry without instantly stripping rank.

Newly witnessed service restores practical battlefield trust quickly: three enemy combat kills, one enemy vehicle knockout, a witnessed objective capture, or a substantial rescue under fire suffice. Lesser assistance contributes proportionally. Unwitnessed deeds, prisoner killings, finishing an already incapacitated soldier, old merit and treating someone deliberately wounded by the player do not buy this restoration. Comrades stop treating the player as hostile, cooperate again and resume still-held command appointments; a debrief appears in T automatically. Fresh deliberate attacks restart the process. Previous reports and unrelated punishment remain; lost ranks and formal sanctions are not automatically erased.

This fast reconciliation is a deliberate gameplay concession, not a historical legal pardon. The 1943 US Army manual distinguishes excusable accidents from culpable negligence and intentional homicide. The game conservatively declines punishment when intent is ambiguous; it does not simulate a full negligence investigation, national courts-martial or the spread of individual disciplinary beliefs through every unit. Old saves did not record intent, so their unsubstantiated friendly-fire counters are archived and no longer justify hostility. Existing witnessed reports from the new system survive reloads.

### Construction, supply and the home front

Projects include foxholes, trenches, sandbags, wire, gun pits, concrete fighting positions, anti-tank obstacles and ditches, camouflage, road and bridge repairs, command/observation posts, supply and aid points, workshops, landing-strip and railhead works, food stores, generators, shelters and water points. Completion alters physical terrain and registers operating installations; destruction of a constructed installation's critical tile disables it.

Labor is measured in man-seconds. A basic foxhole takes approximately two man-hours before skill adjustments, rather than appearing after thirty seconds. Large infrastructure represents a local working/service area, with its strategic effect abstracted; the few runway tiles are not a literal full-length airstrip. Material “crates” are normalized supply units, including stores/equipment delivery, not identical hand-portable boxes. These costs are tuning values, not historical bills of materials.

Factories consume material and fuel. Their output depends on power, surviving local labor, damage and food provision. Civilian relief consumes finite food; disconnected food depots cannot create food. Ordinary troops need carried rations or access to a local distribution point. Supply trucks bring finite ammunition, spares and engineer stores. Fitters can repair a radio in the rear without repairing the hull in the field.

### Intelligence and artillery

On the loaded battlefield, radio sightings reach the shared command picture after a transmission delay. Reports preserve the observed coordinates and time; they do not follow hidden enemies or learn that an unseen enemy died. Both sides use this path. Strategic reports abstract distant friendly units and patrols, with a ten-minute friendly reporting delay and twenty-minute reconnaissance delay, estimates of enemy strength, and a dependency on supply/communication routes. Carried sheets retain older reports until copied at HQ.

Officers and command staff now receive periodic situation packets. A working personal set, their vehicle radio, a radioman within speaking distance or a nearby radio-equipped crew can pass one on, with a thirty-second reception delay. The route is checked at delivery. Without that route, an available real soldier at HQ or with a radio carries a paper packet on foot, then returns. Men on explicit player tasks are not reassigned. A runner can take cover, fight, be wounded or killed, miss a commander who moved from the last signalled position, or fail to find a path. There is no guaranteed delivery timer. Officers of both armies follow these rules; delivered sightings can inform their subordinate command decisions.

Packets freeze the reports available at departure. They do not copy live enemy locations or turn sound marks into precise sightings. Newer handwritten reports survive the arrival of an older packet. Staff summaries show the transport and receipt time; each map report keeps its original observation time. Radio briefings are attempted every two to three minutes when there is new information, so useful updates continue without a stream of duplicate messages. Outlying HQ communications remain part of the strategic reporting abstraction.

Automatic exact retaliation against a firing battery is removed. A loaded battery can be located by an actual radio-equipped observer with recent visual contact, or by two occupied, separated observation posts after repeated firing. The visual path adds at least ninety seconds; surveyed sound ranging adds at least ten minutes and substantial position error. A single sound mark is not a surveyed grid reference. Off-map counterbattery requires prepared observation installations and supply. There must still be a usable gun/battery and ammunition to answer; a player's rank no longer halves response time.

The specified delays, baseline lengths and errors are simulation settings. Two posts are a simplified representation of a surveyed sound/flash-ranging organization, not a claim that two riflemen could reproduce a complete observation battalion. Previously scheduled or prepared barrages may still arrive quickly because they do not need a new target-location cycle.

### Late-war heavy vehicles and crew speech

The German vehicle selection includes the service-used Jagdtiger and Sturmtiger. The Jagdtiger has a 128 mm gun, six modeled crew stations including a second loader, forty rounds and slower handling when a loader is missing. The Sturmtiger has a 380 mm demolition rocket mortar, fourteen HE rounds and a slow reload. Both are rare, slow, fuel-intensive and expensive in ammunition handling time; ordinary damage, crew casualties, repair and supply systems apply. Normal starts use Germany and late-war date restrictions; foreign crews require the captured-equipment setting. These custom assignments are date-compatible possibilities, not claims that either vehicle fought in every selectable late-war battle. Armour ratings, ranges, loading delays and fuel multipliers are game approximations. Prototypes such as the Maus were not added.

Commander steering taps still change direction immediately and give visual acknowledgement. Repeated headings no longer repeat speech, and changed headings have both a fifteen-game-second and eight-real-second voice cooldown. Urgent crew warnings are unaffected.

## Historical grounding

These sources informed the mechanisms and terminology, rather than certifying every tuning value:

- [US War Department, FM 100-5, Operations (22 May 1941)](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/ref/FM/FM100-5/index.html): command, task organization, initiative and flexible application of principles.
- [US War Department, TM-E 30-451, Handbook on German Military Forces, Chapter IV](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/Germany/HB/HB-4.html): subordinate responsibility, reconnaissance instructions, concentration and artillery observation. This is a wartime US assessment, not an original German regulation.
- [US War Department, TM-E 30-480, Handbook on Japanese Military Forces (1944)](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/Japan/IJA/HB/): wartime assessment of Japanese tactical methods, likewise an external assessment.
- [The Field Artillery Journal, September 1944](https://tradocfcoeccafcoepfwprod.blob.core.usgovcloudapi.net/fires-bulletin-archive/1944/SEP_1944/SEP_1944_FULL_EDITION.pdf): counterbattery intelligence from sound/flash ranging, photography, shell reports and observers.
- [US Navy Bureau of Yards and Docks, Building the Navy's Bases in World War II](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USN/Building_Bases/index.html): the construction organizations, work and supporting resources behind naval bases.
- [US Army, World War II NCO history](https://www.army.mil/article/18048/army_nco_history_part_6_world_war_ii): Audie Murphy's advance through enlisted service to a battlefield commission informs the distinction between exceptional service and instant senior command.
- [US Army Manual for Courts-Martial, 1943, paragraphs 148–149](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/ref/MCM/MCM-26.html): accident, malice, culpable negligence and the evidence required for homicide offenses. This is a US reference, not a claim of identical law across all armies.
- [The Tank Museum, Jagdtiger](https://tankmuseum.org/tank-nuts/tank-collection/jagdtiger/): service use, 128 mm armament, six crew and the operational burden of the heavy tank destroyer.
- [The Tank Museum, Sturmtiger](https://tankmuseum.org/article/vehicle-sturmtiger/): the eighteen manufactured vehicles, 380 mm weapon, fourteen-round load and service in Warsaw and Germany. [Its ammunition-handling account](https://tankmuseum.org/article/sturmtigers_fire_power) informs the prolonged loading and supply burden.

## Verification and compatibility

`tests/test_command_realism.py` covers authority at delivery, actual reassignments, loss thresholds, witness survival, broken radios, stale reports, copied maps, material consumption, interrupted works, destroyed HQs, counterbattery observation and repair destinations. `tests/test_vehicle_start.py` covers exact model/seat starts, creator filtering, passenger behavior and save/load.

`tests/test_staff_career.py` exercises physical HQ reports, interrupted interviews and refits, retained promotion credit through the complete rank ladder, delayed decoration presentation, finite support, intelligence hand-ins, mission precedence, radio/vehicle/runner updates for both sides, lost couriers, stale-map protection, key bindings and steering audio throttling. Vehicle-start tests also fire and save/load both new heavy vehicles and check the second loader.

`tests/test_service_navigation.py` covers stable workshop assignments and exit markers, actual driving to the arrival area, sector transitions, X-to-T visits, matching arrows and Enter actions, save/load, contact loss, completion and operational deferral.

`tests/test_friendly_fire.py` exercises vehicle blast and crew attribution, stray damage, separate attacks and warnings, surviving witnesses, delayed explosives, rehabilitation, orders, AI hostility and save migration.

New records are ordinary saved dictionaries with defaults for older saves. Existing maps acquire new intelligence records on the next reporting cycle. Existing earned ranks and medals are retained; unsupported automatic counterbattery events from old saves are discarded. Returning to an older executable after saving new construction/personnel state is not supported.
